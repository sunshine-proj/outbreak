What changed in how enemies fight

Combat moves: in combat, each enemy now picks one of five moves:

retreat;
charge (melee enemies);
flank;
take cover (when under fire or reloading);
fight from its normal range.
It keeps shooting during all of them. The settings live in a new Tactics section on each enemy definition, in OutbreakEnemyTactics.h.

Coordination: nearby enemies share where they last saw you, so one that loses sight keeps tracking you while an ally can see you. Against the same target, one holds the front while up to two others flank. Fire spreads across your squad instead of everyone shooting the player. This is in OutbreakEnemyCoordinationSubsystem.h.

Squad-aware flanking: a flanker picks the side away from your other squad members.

Target switching: an enemy changes target only when another is clearly better.

Retreat: a new Retreating state, triggered once at low health or when outnumbered.

Disengagement: an optional leash keeps an enemy near its home position. If you escape beyond it, the enemy goes home instead of searching.

Melee attacks: a new Melee fire type (a short sweep), plus an unlimited-ammo flag on weapons. Melee enemies attack through the normal weapon and combat code.

Two new archetypes: a fast melee enemy (Stalker) and a heavy (Brute). Both are data only; their assets are described in Docs/Phase9-EditorSetup.md.

Inventory	Each soldier has a weight limit (25 kg by default; roles and loadouts can change it). There is one primary and one secondary weapon slot, plus a selected utility item. Ammunition stays in the existing weapon reserve but counts towards the weight, loaded rounds included.
Pickups	Items lying in the world are picked up with Interact. You take what fits and the rest stays. A weapon whose slot is taken is swapped, leaving yours behind. Mission items can't be dropped.
Loadouts	A loadout asset is a full equipment set, checked for slots, stack limits and weight. Roles, individual teammates and the player can each have one. Loadouts can also be picked from a list in the squad configuration; the selection screen comes with the Phase 11 briefing.
Downed and revive	At zero health, the player and teammates go down and bleed out after 30 s unless revived; further damage kills. Reviving takes 4 s with the reviver staying nearby, and brings the soldier back at 30% health. Each soldier can be revived twice per mission. Medics heal more and revive faster.
Teammate help	Teammates heal themselves when hurt and not under fire. The nearest one goes to revive a downed squadmate (including you), even under an order. Healing a hurt squadmate only happens when they have no order.
Mission	A downed player no longer fails the mission while a teammate is still standing. It fails if you bleed out, or if the whole squad is down.
HUD and input	An inventory summary, plus a full panel on Tab. Downed and revive status lines, and a bleed-out countdown in the squad panel. New keys: heal yourself, throw, cycle utility item, inventory panel.
Changes to earlier behaviour

Interaction now runs on the server: a client sends the request and the server checks range again. Pickups and revives need this. Anything that relied on the interaction happening only on the client, such as the Phase 1 test cube, now changes state only on the server.
Squad soldiers are downed before they die: I updated the two existing tests this affected (the squad panel line and the custom priority-order test). Re-run all earlier tests, because any test that kills a soldier is affected.
New teammate priority: "medical assistance" is now second in the default order, after the teammate's own survival. A teammate AI asset whose priority order was saved before Phase 10 will fall back to the default with a warning; the guide says to reset it.

Reviving while fighting: a reviver must stay within 2 m but can still shoot.

What the new code does:

Day/night: a time-of-day actor turns the sun and moon and sets light levels by hour. Time can pass or stay fixed, and missions can set a start hour. It also feeds the stealth light model, so night is darker for enemies too. Levels without one behave as before.
Environment zones: a box volume that changes enemy perception inside it. It can shorten sight (fog, forest), mask hearing (a noisy generator hall) and make footsteps louder or quieter (metal floor, grass). It can also play a looping ambience for the player inside. For positional sound and reverb, the guide uses the engine's own Ambient Sound and Audio Volume actors.
Doors:
Locks: they swing or slide open, can be locked, and open with a key item or a terminal.
AI: teammates and enemies open unlocked doors by walking into them. Locked doors block AI paths.
Stealth: closed doors block sight and muffle noise, and opening one makes noise enemies can hear.
Equipment: I extended the Phase 11 equipment class rather than adding a new one. It can now be switched off again, makes noise when used, and drives linked doors and lights. Cutting a generator darkens the floodlit yard for stealth.
Destruction: I extended the Phase 11 destructible object. It shows a damaged stage, turns to rubble or disappears (so cover behind it is gone), is loud when destroyed, and can explode and set off other destructibles.
Settings you should know about:

Navigation: I added a setting to Config/DefaultEngine.ini that lets doors change AI navigation at runtime. A navmesh already saved in a level keeps its own value, so it needs setting there too.
Destroyed objects: they don't open new AI paths with that setting; full dynamic navigation would, but costs more.
Teammates: zones only affect enemy perception, not teammates.

an order acknowledged;
first contact;
reloading;
a squadmate down or killed, by name;

an objective done.
